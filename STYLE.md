# Kuku Malayalam audio stories: writing rules (from Kavya + native reader, Oct 2026)

Length: 585-650 story words per episode (4:30-5:00 at 130 wpm). Never pad.
Where length comes from: in-scene action, what people do and say, fuller spoken exchanges
built from the source beats. NEVER from the narrator explaining.

Banned, because readers caught them:
1. Telling feelings the scene already shows ("അവൾക്ക് സങ്കടം തോന്നി", "പ്രതീക്ഷ ഇല്ലാതായി",
   "അവന് മനസ്സിലായി", "അവൾ ഓർത്തു"). Show the reaction in an action or a line of dialogue.
2. Summing up after a strong line of dialogue. Let the line land; cut to the next action.
3. Narrator questions ("ഇവനു വേണ്ടിയായിരുന്നോ?").
4. Hints that spoil a later reveal (Antony being rich before "Good evening Boss").
5. English idioms in Malayalam words ("കളി മാറി", "നെഞ്ച് നീറി", "ഭാരം കനത്തു").
6. Long sentences: one sentence = one picture. No semicolons. Max ~22 words of narration.
7. Filler connectors that add nothing: പക്ഷേ/എന്നാൽ at sentence start, ഇപ്പോഴും, കുറച്ചു കഴിഞ്ഞപ്പോൾ.
8. Every paragraph the same shape. Vary: some paragraphs are two sentences, some are action only.
Keep: spoken dialogue, simple past-tense narration, English words in Roman with hyphen suffixes,
subheading on line 2, all cues untouched.

## Native-voice rules (ported from the Tamil pipeline, ~/.claude/script-pipeline/skills/narration_style.md)
9. Build the thought in Malayalam. If a line feels translated, re-say the idea; never word-swap.
10. Subject first. No English-style participle opening ("X ചെയ്തുകൊണ്ട് അലീന..." before the subject).
11. Flow = spoken connectors (അപ്പോഴാണ്, അതോടെ, പിന്നെ, അങ്ങനെ, പക്ഷേ sparingly), not long sentences.
12. Scene-setting in ONE breath, not a row of noun fragments. Short punchy line only for the turn.
13. No recap/label sentence after a scene shows something. No noun-heavy calques ("X-ന് ആഗ്രഹം ഉണ്ടായിരുന്നു" -> verb).
14. Invented dialogue: almost none. Every quote names its speaker in the same sentence (audio has no faces).
